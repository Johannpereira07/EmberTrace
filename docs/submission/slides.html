The Multi-Decade Challenge

The Multi-Sensor Discontinuity Dilemma

1. Resolution Mismatch

MODIS (~1 km) vs VIIRS (375 m).
VIIRS detects smaller, cooler, and nighttime fires that MODIS systematically misses.

2. Changing Satellites

1 satellite in 2000 (Terra) → 2 in 2002 (Aqua joins) → up to 5 in 2023. Raw counts spike with satellite launches, independent of real fire activity.

3. The 2012 Fake Jump

Merging raw counts creates a false +118% jump in 2012. Users cannot tell whether fires doubled or if the camera just got sharper.

⚠️ Why Simple Averaging Fails

Averaging sensors blends different sensitivities and creates a downward step when older sensors decommission. A true continuous baseline requires physical harmonization to VIIRS equivalence.

Methodology & Architecture

The 7-Stage Harmonization Engine

⚙️ Processing Flow (`harmonize.py`)
  1. Load & Unify: Identify sensor from satellite name; unify schemas.
  2. Filter: Drop low confidence; empirical static flare mask for NRT.
  3. Grid & Count: 0.25° cells per month; quiet cells filled with 0.
  4. Normalize: Divide counts by active satellites (accounting for NASA outage days).
  5. Calibrate: Log1p regression on drift-free afternoon orbits (2012–2019) + bias factor.
  6. Harmonize: VIIRS (2012+), Calibrated Aqua (2002–11), Chained Terra (2000–02).
  7. Calendar: 26-year monthly totals and anomaly ratios vs medians.
🔬 Key Scientific Innovations
  • Afternoon Orbit Matching: Aqua and VIIRS share 1:30 PM overpasses, matching peak diurnal burning without time-of-day bias.
  • Two-Stage Chaining: Terra (morning) chained through Aqua to achieve 2000–2002 coverage.
  • Bias Factor Correction: Fixes systematic log-transform underestimation.
  • No Post-2012 Blending: VIIRS alone defines the modern gold standard.
